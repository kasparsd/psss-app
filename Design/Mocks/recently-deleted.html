Recently Deleted

Mock for approval. Nothing here is built. Four screens are new: the list, the item on its way out, the two confirmations for deleting for good, and the empty state. One thing changes elsewhere: deleting an item no longer asks first, and offers Undo instead.

What a first-time person must know here.
  • Which thing? Items they deleted from this vault, in a place called Recently Deleted. Not a separate folder or file: they are still inside the encrypted vault file, under the same vault password.
  • What can they do? Restore an item, or delete it for good, one at a time or all at once.
  • What can go wrong? The vault file was changed by another app or device since Psss read it, so the restore is refused. This is the existing conflict alert, reused word for word.
  • How do they leave? Nothing here changes unless they press a labeled button. After 30 days Psss removes items for good, and says the date on every row.
  • Who else sees them? Other KeePass apps show these items in their Recycle Bin and never remove them. One sentence says so, in one place under the list.

Dates assume today is 23 Nov 2026. “Removed for good on” is deleted date plus 30 days; Psss removes the item when the vault is next opened on or after that day.

1. Reached from, and the list

iPad follows the Mac layout: in a wide window the sidebar, list and detail columns are the same as the Mac frames; in a narrow window or portrait the sidebar hides and the pill row from the iPhone frames sits above the list, with Recently Deleted as its last pill. Confirmations appear as a popover anchored to the button that was pressed (Delete Now… or Delete All…) instead of a bottom sheet; the words are identical. The conflict alert is the centered system alert.

2. Deleting an item elsewhere in the app

iPad: the capsule sits at the bottom of the detail column as on iPhone, and ⌘Z works with a hardware keyboard.

Decisions for the owner

  1. Recently Deleted is the last row of the vault section in the sidebar, and the last pill on iPhone.
    Rejected: A Filters entry (Filters are traits of live items; this holds items that are gone). A row in Setup (hidden, and an action people need in a panic). Search scope (not discoverable).
    Why: It is already a scope of the same list in shared code, Apple Notes and Photos put it last in the folder list, and the pill row costs nothing on iPhone. Its risk is that the pill is off screen at launch; the Undo capsule covers the first minutes, and the footer on the empty state says where it lives.
  2. Deleting an item has no confirmation. The row reads “Delete Item”, and a capsule with Undo (10 seconds) follows. Mac adds Edit, Undo Delete Item.
    Rejected: Keep “Delete Item…” with a confirmation (it now asks about something that can be undone, and teaches people to click through). A modal Undo alert (blocks the list).
    Why: The experience doc already says every destructive action is a move with Undo, except bulk deletes, and Apple says not to confirm undoable actions.
  3. Deleting for good always confirms, for one item and for all, with the count on the button for all.
    Rejected: No confirmation for one item (it is the one irreversible step). Typing the word “delete” to confirm (too heavy for 4 items).
    Why: Irreversible and password data: the cost of one extra tap is small. Backup copies are named because the file's rollback copies may still hold the item.
  4. The bulk button is called “Delete All…”, not “Empty”.
    Rejected: “Empty” (Mail and Trash wording; works only if people think of a bin). “Delete Everything” (loud).
    Why: It says what happens to the items. The brief called it Empty; this is a wording suggestion, easy to flip.
  5. An item in Recently Deleted can be opened read-only, with Show and Copy for secrets, but not edited.
    Rejected: Restore and Delete Now only, no way to look (people need to confirm it is the right item, or copy a password they just removed). Fully editable (confusing: it is deleted).
    Why: Looking is harmless and often the reason they came; changing a deleted item makes no sense, so “restore it first” is the one sentence.
  6. A failed Restore reuses the existing conflict alert unchanged.
    Rejected: A new message for restore. A silent retry.
    Why: One concept, one wording, and Save My Change Anyway does the right thing for a restore too. It names no item (see page 4 for the wording I would change).
  7. Rows say “Deleted 3 days ago” and the absolute date “removed for good on 20 Dec”.
    Rejected: Absolute only. Relative only (goes stale on screen).
    Why: The brief asks for both. The absolute date is the one that matters and does not go stale; the relative one answers “was it me?”. This departs from items.md, which avoids relative wording for dates.
  8. The sentence about other KeePass apps sits once, in the list footer and in the empty state.
    Rejected: On every row, in Setup, in the confirmation.
    Why: UI-clarity: help text in one place. The confirmation says only what is true about that action.

Conflicts with Apple's guidance

  1. Undo capsule on iOS.
    iOS has no standard toast. Apple's undo guidance is shake to undo and the Edit menu, and neither is discoverable on iPhone. The labeled capsule is visible and tappable; the trade-off is a custom component that disappears after 10 seconds. VoiceOver users get the announcement plus an Undo action.
  2. A pill, not a list row, on iPhone.
    Apple Notes and Photos list Recently Deleted in a folder list. Psss iPhone has no folder list by design (search-first, already a recorded departure), so the last pill is the closest fit.
  3. Delete Now… and Delete All… are labeled text buttons in content and the nav bar, not an icon-only trash.
    Apple's toolbars would use a trash symbol. The project rule is visible, labeled actions; the cost is more space in the navigation bar.
  4. Confirmation as a bottom sheet on iPhone and an alert on Mac.
    This follows the HIG (action sheet on iPhone, alert on Mac). On iPad it should become a popover, which the mock only describes.
