Fill a login

Drawn 24 Nov 2026 from the code as it is today (frames 1 to 8 and sections 5 to 7): strings are copied from Extensions/CredentialProvider, App/Core/FillSite.swift, SiteMatch.swift and PasskeyRequestList.swift, so the owner can see the whole flow in one place and change it. Sections 3 and 4 and “The stories” were approved by the owner on 24 Nov 2026 and built the same day, with the three differences listed in the History (not yet run on a device; redrawn at the owner’s request): action words are buttons, “Other Logins…” is gone in favor of a search on the same screen, and a person can use one value for the field they tapped. One owner decision is also not built, and is marked where it appears: a suggestion fills straight after Face ID, with no “Fill” screen (decision 7). The system’s own pieces (the suggestion above the keyboard, the Face ID prompt, the AutoFill menu) are not ours and are drawn only as far as I know them. Not viewed on a device.

0. What a first-time person must know

  • Which thing? One login saved in Psss, put into the sign-in page the person is looking at. Psss offers only logins saved for that site. Others sit behind a labeled step with a confirmation.
  • What must they give? Face ID or Touch ID, once per fill. If more than one login fits, one tap on its Fill button. To use one value, such as a card number in a mislabeled field, one tap on the field, then on the value.
  • What can go wrong? Nothing is saved for this site; Face ID is cancelled or was turned off; the vault file cannot be found or opened; the page is not encrypted; the page is not the site the login was saved for.
  • Where does it live? The logins stay in the vault on this device. Nothing is sent to a server. The page receives only the one login the person chose.
  • How do they leave? Cancel on every screen. The page is left as it was and nothing in the vault changes.
  • What happens next? The email and password fields are filled. The person presses the page's own sign-in button; Psss never submits the page.

The system, not Psss, decides what shows above the keyboard or under the field. Psss is called only after the person chooses it. If AutoFill is off none of this happens (Turning on AutoFill, frame 19).

The stories: three moments

Walked in order, like the AutoFill setup page. Each story names the person, what they want, and what they see. The first is the everyday fill and is drawn in section 2; the other two are new and share the screens in sections 3 and 4.

Story 1: the usual

Kid opens heron-bank.example and taps the password field. Psss is listed above the keyboard; Kid taps it, Face ID, and the email and password are in. No screen of ours between Face ID and the filled page (decision 7). Drawn in section 2, frames 1 to 4. Nothing here changed.

Story 2: the bank moved its sign-in

Blue Heron Bank now lives at heron-bank.example, but the login in Psss was saved for heron-bank-old.example. Kid taps the password field, taps the Passwords key above the keyboard, chooses Psss and passes Face ID. Psss finds nothing for the new address. Kid does not edit anything: types “heron” into the search that is already on the screen, opens the right item, and sees both addresses side by side before choosing Fill Username and Password.

Story 3: the form calls the card number “Full name”

At shop.example the checkout’s first field is labeled “Full name” but takes the card number. System AutoFill would offer a name. Kid taps the field and chooses Psss’s text insertion (iPhone and iPad, iOS 18 and later; frame 14). Face ID, then the search; Kid types “visa”, opens the card, and presses Use on Card number. The page’s label never mattered: the field Kid tapped decides where the value goes. On Mac the same story ends with Copy and ⌘V.

After Face ID. Changed 24 Nov 2026 at the owner’s request (his two screenshots: “Fill” read as text, and “Other Logins…” led to a second screen of rows). Every action is now a button with a shape. 4. Fill one field, not the whole login (built 24 Nov 2026; not run on a device)

Added 24 Nov 2026 at the owner’s request, extended the same day. The question: a form labels the card number “Full name”, so the system’s AutoFill would offer a name. The person needs to say which value goes in the field they are looking at.

How it works, and what Psss cannot do. iOS tells Psss the site, never which field is tapped, so Psss cannot aim a value at a field. The person aims it: they tap the page’s own field first, then choose one value in Psss, and that value goes into the field they tapped. Two ways in, both ending on the same screen:

  • From the picker (frames 5 to 8): tap a login’s name, not its Fill button, to open its values.
  • From the field (frame 14, iOS 18 and later): tap the field, choose Psss’s text insertion, search, choose the item. This route is the one built for the card-number case. Whether the system allows it from the Passwords key too is open question 1 and needs a device.

On the Mac there is no way for a provider to insert one value, so the button says Copy and the person pastes (frames 10 and 11). The search includes cards for this reason: a mislabeled checkout is exactly when the card number is needed (open question 3).

5. Another site, or a page that is not encrypted

One sheet of Psss’s own, on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Cancel is the default and answers Return; Fill Anyway is a plain button, not red, because continuing is not a deletion. Values from the vault are in quotes and site names are not, so one cannot pass for the other.

7. Verification codes

A code is offered only for logins that have a readable code and belong to the site. There is no “Other Logins” for codes, by design: a code for the wrong site is worse than none.

Decisions for the owner

  1. Psss fills only logins saved for the page’s site, and the rest sit behind “Other Logins…” and a confirmation naming both sites.
    Rejected: Listing every login (one tap away from filling a bank password on a lookalike page). Hiding other logins entirely (a login saved under a different address could never be used).
    Why: the confirmation names both sites, so the person sees the mismatch. This is as built.
  2. Every action in the picker is a button with a shape (Fill, Fill…, Use, Copy); the “Other Logins…” step is replaced by a search on the same screen (owner request, 2026-11-24; proposed, not built).
    Rejected: plain blue text as the action (the owner could not tell it was tappable; Apple’s own guidance for a visible action is a button, not a bare label). A search that opens only after tapping a button (a screen and a tap whose only job is to reach the search). Listing every other login under the site’s own (one tap from a bank password on a lookalike page, which decision 1 refuses). Opening the keyboard at once (it covers half the list).
    Why: the button is what people tap; the search sits where the old button was, and other items appear only when the person types, so decision 1 still holds. The iOS 26 search pill floats at the bottom of the screen and rows can scroll under it; the list gets bottom space so the last row is never hidden.
  3. Proposed, not built: the popup searches the whole vault, and tapping a result’s name opens the item instead of filling it (owner request, 2026-11-24).
    Rejected: asking the person to edit the item's site first (a changed address would stop them signing in until they found the item in the app). Searching only logins by name and account (the old address is what they remember). Filling the whole login at the first tap on a result (no way to use one value).
    Why: a service that moves its sign-in must not turn the vault into a locked box. Search covers name, account and saved site, over logins, custom items and cards (the card number case needs cards). Notes are left out (open question 3).
  4. The item screen offers the whole login first and single values below it, with Use where the system inserts and Copy where it cannot (proposed, not built).
    Rejected: only single values (two taps and two popups for the common case). Hiding single values behind a menu (a person who needs one would not find it).
    Why: the first button is what a changed address needs; the rows answer the “which value” request. The verb names what happens: Use inserts, Copy copies.
  5. The item screen carries the two sites and is the confirmation (proposed, not built). Recommended; the owner decides.
    Rejected: a separate “Fill a login from another site?” sheet after the choice (a third screen, and the two sites would be read twice). Hiding the sites on this screen and asking only after (the choice would be made before the person has seen the mismatch).
    Why: the decision and the facts are on one screen. Cost: it breaks today's rule that Cancel is the prominent default, because there are several actions. Back is the way out, and nothing fills on a stray tap on a row because each row's action word is visible. The http line is shown on this screen too.
  6. The person aims a value by tapping the page’s field first; Psss never guesses which field a value is for (proposed, not built).
    Rejected: asking the person to pick a field name inside Psss (the system tells a provider the site, never the field, so any choice would be a guess shown as fact). Trusting the page’s labels (the mislabeled form is the very case).
    Why: the field the person tapped is the only reliable fact. Costs: iPhone and iPad from iOS 18 only, and not yet confirmed to work from the Passwords key (open question 1); on Mac the value is copied and pasted.
  7. Psss never changes the item from here.
    Rejected: adding the new site to the login automatically (anything the person finds could be tied to a lookalike page by one tap).
    Why: filling and editing are different acts. A later offer to remember the site is open question 2.
  8. Cancel is the default on the confirmation; Fill Anyway is plain, not red.
    Rejected: a system alert (cannot make Cancel both the Return default and the Escape button).
    Why: as built; continuing is not a deletion.
  9. A suggestion fills straight after Face ID, with no confirmation screen (owner decision, 2026-11-24; not built: frame 3 draws today’s screen).
    Rejected: a “Fill” screen after Face ID (it repeated the name and account already in the suggestion the person tapped, and Apple Passwords fills straight away). A confirmation stays only where it adds a fact the person cannot see: another site, or an unencrypted page, both on the list path (frames 15 and 16). The suggestion path cannot meet either case: the system gives only the stored domain with it, and the release check refuses a login whose site does not match.
    Why: one fewer tap on the most common fill, and no screen that only restates what was just chosen. Not built yet: FillConfirm and its use in prepareInterfaceToProvideCredential go away (see “What it needs from the code”).
  10. Face ID before any list, even an empty one.
    Rejected: showing “No logins are saved” before Face ID (leaks that nothing is saved without proving who is asking).
    Why: as built; the vault has to be opened to know.
  11. The sample vault is never offered to AutoFill.
    Rejected: filling sample logins (they are fake and would fill real pages with fake passwords).
    Why: as built (“No vault yet”).
  12. Nothing about AutoFill being off is shown here.
    Rejected: a message from the AutoFill sheet (the sheet never runs while AutoFill is off).
    Why: the reminder card has to do it, before the person leaves the app.

What it needs from the code

  • The picker (proposed): FillList loses its “Other Logins…” screen and button: one screen, the site’s logins with a bordered Fill button per row, and a search (.searchable on iOS, a text field on Mac) whose results show only after typing. A name tap opens the item; the Fill button fills at once (with the other-site question for a result from another site).
  • Find an item and its fields (new, proposed): a pure search in App/Core (name, account, saved site; logins, custom items and cards; A to Z; the count for “Showing 40 of …”), and a pure field list for an item (label, a safe preview, whether it is secret), both with hostless tests. The provider's others list is replaced by them.
  • ProvidesTextToInsert in the provider's Info.plist, prepareInterfaceForUserChoosingTextToInsert (iOS 18, not Mac) and completeRequest(withTextToInsert:) for Use. On Mac, and wherever insertion is refused, the clipboard with the concealed, 60-second rules already used for item copies.
  • ReleaseCheck gains a per-field release: the one chosen value, nothing else, and the other-site facts computed the same way as the old sheet.
  • In CredentialProviderViewController.prepareInterfaceToProvideCredential(for: ASPasswordCredentialIdentity): after the release check, call complete(with: item) directly instead of presenting FillConfirm. Remove FillConfirm from ProviderViews.swift if nothing else uses it (the passkey and code identity paths do not).
  • Keep the release check (ReleaseCheck.password): it is what makes skipping the screen safe. Add a check that a login whose site does not match the identity is refused with no screen (K2 covers the rule; the wiring is the gap).
  • Frames 1 to 3 and F2 change in the next build; the iPhone and Mac screens in sections 3 to 6 do not.

Conflicts with Apple's guidance

  1. Copy instead of Use on Mac. macOS gives providers no call to insert one value (completeRequestWithTextToInsert is iOS 18 and later only, checked in the 27.2 SDK headers). Trade-off: the person pastes, and the clipboard holds the value for 60 seconds.
  2. A confirmation that is also the item screen. Apple's alerts have a default and a cancel button. Trade-off in decision 4.
  3. Psss’s own sheet instead of a system alert for other-site and http confirmations. Trade-off: a custom component to keep, in exchange for a safe default on every platform.

Open questions

  1. Can a password list request insert one value, and how does a person reach “Choose Text to Insert”? As built, the answer is assumed to be no: the item screen from the picker says Copy on iPhone too, and only the text-insertion route says Use. One flag flips it once a device shows otherwise. The SDK header says prepareInterfaceForUserChoosingTextToInsert serves a separate text-insertion request on iOS 18 and later, and does not say whether completeRequest(withTextToInsert:) may be called from the password list. If it may not, frames 10 and 11 on iPhone use Copy as on Mac and the entrance is only frame 14. Device only (K12).
  2. After a fill from another site, offer “Also offer this login on heron-bank.example”? It would save the person the next search. It edits the item from a screen that has no edit mode, and it ties the login to a site they had to be sure of. Recommended: not in this change; the person edits the site in the app, and the search is the bridge.
  3. Should search include cards and notes? Changed 24 Nov 2026: recommended now includes cards, because a mislabeled checkout (story 3) needs the card number and nothing else would let a person use it. This is only “use one value”, not filling a payment form, which still waits on the checkout work in autofill.md. A card’s values are released only after Face ID and one at a time. A note has no field to use. Recommended: logins, custom items and cards.
  4. Should “No logins are saved for this site” offer to add one? The search is on the screen, so the person can use another item. Without a login they still have a dead end. A person who has no login yet gets a dead end. Saving happens on sign-up (password generator), not here.
  5. Should the list show what the person last used first? With several logins for one site (frame 6) the order is the vault’s. A recent-use order exists elsewhere (Recently used).
  6. When Face ID fails or is cancelled, is silence enough (F11)? The system sheet closes and the page is unchanged, so the person may not know whether anything happened. Apple’s own flow behaves the same.
