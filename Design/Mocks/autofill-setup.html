Turning on AutoFill

Approved 23 Nov 2026, with one change. The owner’s words: “for the initial onboarding, after creating the vault the user already gets the Face ID unlock request so it seems like placing the prompt inline (like no 6, option a) is the best approach. don’t want to push too many decisions right away.” So there is no separate step after the vault is created: section 1 (frames 1 to 5 as a step or sheet) is not built and stays here only as the record of what was rejected. The card of section 2, option A (frames 6, 7 and 8), is the onboarding, including in a new empty vault. Its button asks through Apple’s prompt the first time and opens Settings after that, with the steps in words inside the card (the wording of frames 4 and 16). Sections 3 to 6 stand as drawn. Frames are unchanged.

Mock drawn 23 Nov 2026, approved the same day. The card, the confirmation, Later (drawn here first as Hide) and Setup are what gets built; the step is not. It answers one thing from the owner’s first run on a real iPhone: after creating a vault, nothing said that AutoFill still has to be switched on in Settings, so Safari offered nothing. Not viewed rendered: this page was written without a browser, so check it in one, in light and dark.

What a first-time person must know here. The person is the owner’s family, moving from 1Password or Apple Passwords.
  • Which thing? Two jobs: Psss holds the logins, and the system fills them. The system needs one permission, called AutoFill, in Settings. Psss cannot give it to itself.
  • Why now, what for? Without it nothing fills anywhere, in Safari or in any app. The person has just made a vault, so this is the moment the word makes sense.
  • Where does it live? The permission is a device setting, not part of the vault. It is per device, and each iPhone, iPad and Mac needs it once.
  • Who holds the key? Not relevant here. Nothing about the vault key changes, and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • How do they leave? Every screen has a labeled way out. Nothing is lost by skipping: AutoFill stays off, and a calm reminder says so until it is on.
  • What happens next? The system asks once, they say yes, and a line says what to try: open a website and tap the password field.
Recommended flow. One step right after the Face ID question: “Turn On AutoFill”. If the person says Not Now, a quiet card at the top of the list says “AutoFill is off” with the same button, until AutoFill is on. The card can be hidden for good on this device, and Setup always shows the state. Coming back from Settings, the app notices and confirms once, in place. No alerts, no badges, no notifications at any point.

1. Option A: one step right after the vault is created

Order, one question at a time: create the vault, then the Face ID question (existing, unchanged), then this step. The step never shows if AutoFill is already on, for example after a reinstall.

iPad: the step is a sheet, as on the Mac when wide and as on iPhone when narrow. Words are the same with “iPad”.

2. A calm reminder while AutoFill is off

Not Now has to lead somewhere, or the problem comes back. Three places were compared. Option A is recommended; B and C are drawn so the choice is visible.

Option A (recommended): a card at the top of the list, same on every device

Option B: nothing on the list, only in Setup and the empty vault

Option C: a footer row in the Mac sidebar, a dot on the gear on iPhone

How the card goes away. Only one thing removes it permanently: AutoFill being on. Later removes it for 7 days on this device, then the card returns if AutoFill is still off. Seeing AutoFill on clears the snooze, so a later switch-off brings the card back too (section 5). Setup is where anyone finds it again: it always shows the state and the button.

3. Setup: status, separate from the action

Status is a passive line, and the actions are separate rows, as the toolbar and status rule asks. It replaces today's text “AutoFill fills your logins in apps and websites. You turn it on in Settings.” beside one button.

4. Coming back from Settings

The app checks the state when it becomes active, and on every launch and unlock. If it was off and is now on, the card turns into one confirmation, in place, once.

5. Turned off again later

The person chose another app's AutoFill, or reset settings. Psss sees it on the next launch or when it becomes active. The snooze is cleared whenever AutoFill is seen on, so the card can return.

6. When there is nothing to fill

7. Mac specifics

  • Where: System Settings, General, AutoFill & Passwords. The step, card and Setup all say this in these words on Mac. macOS 15 has the prompt call; macOS 14 does not, but the recommended floor is 15.
  • Local build: an unsigned or unprovisioned build cannot be enabled as an AutoFill provider, so it will show “AutoFill is off” and the card forever, and the prompt will not appear. That is correct behavior and the screens are already honest about it. I recommend no developer-only text in the app. A line in CONTRIBUTING.md saying so is enough.
  • Safari extension: separate from AutoFill. Turning it on is a different setting and is not part of this flow. Not covered here.

9. The system's side: where the switch is

Added 24 Nov 2026. Everything above ends in one place the app does not draw: the system's AutoFill page. These frames show it, so the person's whole path can be read from the card to the switch and back. They are not Psss screens and cannot be changed by us. They are drawn from memory of the system, not from a device, so the labels are approximate. What the app can promise is only the path it names in words: Settings, General, AutoFill & Passwords, then switch on Psss (AutoFillCopy.steps).

  • If the system’s own master switch is off, the list of apps may be hidden or inactive, so the card’s “switch on Psss” has nothing to switch. The app cannot read that (one flag only, section 0), so its words stay correct only if the person looks at the whole page. Open question 5 asks whether the steps should say “and switch on AutoFill Passwords and Passkeys if it is off”.
  • If Psss is not in the list at all, the build lacks the AutoFill capability (ad-hoc or unsigned builds). That is a developer state with no copy by design (decision 10).
  • Another app’s AutoFill is the one in use (a person who uses a browser’s own manager): Psss can be on in this list and still not be the one a browser asks. Nothing to do in Psss; Safari and Chrome ask differently (AutoFill and browser integration).

Moves between states (the app checks at launch, after unlock, whenever it becomes active, and right after a prompt or a Settings call returns):

  • S2 → S3: the person presses the button once. The system prompt appears; whatever the answer, the prompt counts as used. The next press opens Settings, never a second prompt.
  • S2, S3, S4 → S5: Later. A line says Psss will remind in a week and that AutoFill stays in Setup. S5 → S6 by time alone, only while AutoFill is still off.
  • S2, S3, S4, S5, S6 → S7: the person switches it on in Settings (or says yes to the prompt). The snooze and the “prompt used” memory are cleared, and the vault’s logins are registered with the system straight away, so ones saved while AutoFill was off are offered without the next unlock.
  • S7, S8 → S4: the person switches it off later. Setup shows it at once on the next reading, and the snooze is gone, so the card may return immediately.
  • No vault open (including the first run): no card in any state; Setup still shows the state. Any → S9 and back: an unreadable reading changes nothing that is remembered.
  • Settings did not open (any state): an alert says so and gives the steps in words (frame 14). It is the only alert in the flow.

13. AutoFill without Face ID or Touch ID

Owner decision, 2026-11-24: one setting, on by default, no question asked. Psss saves the vault’s key after every password unlock, and the system asks for Face ID or Touch ID when AutoFill reads it, falling back to the device passcode when biometrics are not enrolled or fail. The vault password is never typed in the provider: its password hashing needs 64 to 128 MiB of memory and the provider’s limit is unmeasured. What a person must know: AutoFill asks for Face ID or the passcode each time it fills; the vault password is not stored; the switch deletes the saved key; the vault file is unchanged. Adding a face or fingerprint no longer invalidates the key; anyone who knows the passcode can fill (see decision 1).

14. Mac: turn on the Safari extension

The Mac card. The iPhone and iPad reminder is on its own page, Safari extension reminder. Same card as AutoFill, below it, and only once AutoFill is on, so there is one reminder at a time. The button opens Safari Settings on the Extensions page with Psss selected. Later snoozes for a week, like AutoFill. Switched off in the code until the extension does something for a real vault.

Decisions for the owner

  1. One switch, on by default, and the device passcode as the fallback for Face ID (owner, 2026-11-24).
    Rejected: A second “Fill with device passcode” switch beside Face ID (two choices, and choosing neither leaves AutoFill unable to open the vault). A password field in the provider (Argon2 needs 64 to 128 MiB, the provider’s limit is unmeasured, and a key file cannot be typed). Keeping the key with no check (anyone holding an unlocked phone could fill). Asking before saving (the saving needs no check; the check comes when AutoFill reads the key).
    Why: it works on a phone with no Face ID and adds no question. Cost: the key was bound to the enrolled faces and fingers, so adding one invalidated it; now it does not, and anyone who knows the device passcode can fill logins. That person can already reach Apple Passwords and iCloud Keychain, so this matches the platform, and the footer says what the key does.
  2. Ask in one step right after the Face ID question, with a Not Now, and show the system prompt from it.
    Rejected: Putting it first, before Face ID (two permission questions at once). Skipping the step and relying on the card (the person never meets the word before it matters). Sending them to Settings first (nothing in the app has said why).
    Why: It is the moment the person understands the point, and the system prompt keeps them in the app. Apple allows asking in onboarding when the app depends on it. One question at a time stays true.
  3. The step never shows when AutoFill is already on, and never asks the system twice by itself.
    Rejected: Asking on every launch until yes (pressure, and the system may refuse). Asking again after a No (same).
    Why: A second ask is nagging. After one answer, the plain steps and the Settings button are what is left.
  4. A card at the top of the list while AutoFill is off, same on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
    Rejected: Only the empty vault and Setup (a person who taps Not Now never learns it again). A badge on the gear (Apple limits badges to notification counts, and a dot reads as breakage). A Mac-only sidebar footer (iPhone cannot have it).
    Why: It is seen on the screen the person uses most, it is passive text with a button, and it is one piece of shared code. It disappears by itself when AutoFill turns on.
  5. The card can be snoozed for 7 days on this device with Later, and returns after that if AutoFill is still off, or sooner if AutoFill is seen on and then off.
    Rejected: Never hideable (a card that stays forever teaches people to ignore the list). Hide for a week and return (a timer is a nag). Reversed by the owner on 23 Nov 2026: Later now does exactly this, for 7 days.
    Why: A person who chose not to use AutoFill, such as one who prefers the browser extension, should not see it daily. Setup keeps the state and button, so nothing is lost.
  6. No alert, no notification, no badge and no sound, at any point.
    Rejected: A notification asking to turn it on (needs another permission to explain the first, and Apple says not to send repeats). An alert on launch (Apple says not to alert on launch).
    Why: An alert is for something that went wrong right now. AutoFill off is a state, so it is shown in place.
  7. Settings shows the state as plain text and the action as a row of its own, both in one group.
    Rejected: A switch (it looks like it controls AutoFill, which Psss cannot do). A green “On” (a branded accent is not allowed).
    Why: Plain text beside an accent-colour row keeps status and action apart, and one group shows the button belongs to that status. The label “AutoFill is on” cannot be mistaken for a button.
  8. On becoming active the app checks again and, if AutoFill came on, the card becomes a one-time confirmation that says what to try.
    Rejected: A silent disappearance (the person is left unsure it worked). A sheet (heavy for good news).
    Why: The person is returning from Settings and wants proof. The next step, open a website and tap the password field, is the part nobody tells them.
  9. When the state cannot be read, say so in Setup and show no card.
    Rejected: Treat as off (false alarm). Hide everything (leaves no way in).
    Why: Never claim what Psss cannot see.
  10. No reminder before a vault exists; the sample vault shows it like any other (owner, 2026-11-24).
    Rejected: An exception for the sample vault.
    Why: No special case to keep, and the sample is how a new person first meets the card.
  11. No developer-only text for unsigned Mac builds.
    Rejected: A “this build cannot use AutoFill” line.
    Why: It would be one more string to maintain for nobody the app serves. CONTRIBUTING.md says it.
  12. The state map (section 10) and the checks (section 11) are part of this page, and a change to a frame updates the row that names it.
    Rejected: A separate test plan document (it drifts from the frames). Checking only by looking at the running app (nothing fails when a state is lost).
    Why: The product owner changes frames; the code and the checks must follow from the same page.
  13. Passkeys and verification codes are not reported separately; Setup says what Psss cannot see.
    Rejected: Showing three states (the app cannot read them).
    Why: The state call has one flag. A footer is honest, and a second button is added only if a device check shows the switch exists.

Conflicts with Apple's guidance

  1. A permission step in onboarding, before the person has used the app.
    Apple: if the app needs the resource before it works, asking in onboarding is acceptable; otherwise ask on first use. Psss works without AutoFill, so this leans on the first reading. Trade-off: one more screen before the first item, softened by Not Now and by showing nothing when AutoFill is already on.
  2. Steps for using Settings, shown after a decline.
    Apple says not to teach people how to use the system. Psss cannot turn AutoFill on and the system prompt may not return, so the steps are the only way. Trade-off: some system teaching in the app, shown only after a No and only in the one place it is needed.
  3. A custom card in the content, not a standard component.
    iOS has no standard setup banner in lists. Apple's guidance on informational messages prefers contextual, nonmodal text over alerts, which this follows. Trade-off: a custom component and Later logic, in exchange for no interruption.
  4. Later: snooze for 7 days (first drawn as Hide for good).
    Apple says to let people skip onboarding and find it again in settings or help. This does that, with Setup as the place. Trade-off: a person can hide it and then forget, which is their choice.
  5. Alerts for the Settings-did-not-open error only.
    Apple says to alert about problems and not for plain information. This is a problem after a button press, so an alert is right. Trade-off: none beyond the interruption.
  6. A sheet on Mac.
    Mac apps tend to avoid first-run sheets and show things in the window. A sheet is used because the step is one decision that blocks nothing else. Trade-off: it covers the empty vault until answered; Return and Escape both leave it.

What it needs from the code

  • State: one shared check, ASCredentialIdentityStore.shared.getState, returning on or off. The result is kept in memory with a time, so every screen reads the same value.
  • When it runs: on launch, on unlock, and when the app becomes active (scene phase), and right after the system prompt or Settings call returns. There is no change notification.
  • Ask once: ASSettingsHelper.requestToTurnOnCredentialProviderExtension from the step or the card, the first time only. Respect the 10 second wait: never call twice in that window.
  • Open Settings: the existing openCredentialProviderAppSettings, with its error shown as in frame 14. openVerificationCodeAppSettings only if the device check says it is useful.
  • Stored, per device, not synced: “the system prompt has been shown” and “reminder hidden”. AutoFill is a device setting, so another device must not inherit either answer. Stored in local app preferences, not in the vault file. The snooze date is cleared when the state is seen on.
  • Where the card shows: any open, unlocked vault (the sample too), state off, not hidden. Not before a vault exists. One shared view for iPhone, iPad and Mac, placed in the list column.
  • Text changes: Setup's AutoFill section becomes status plus actions (frames 11 to 13). The empty vault's last sentence changes as in frame 7.
  • Not needed: notification permission, background work and any server.

Open questions

  1. Later for good, or until the next launch? (Decided: neither. Later snoozes for 7 days.) I first recommended for good, with Setup as the way back. The owner chose a snooze instead: the card returns after 7 days while AutoFill is still off, and the stored value is the date Later was chosen.
  2. Does a No to the system prompt block a second one? The header does not say. If the system always shows it again, the card could always call the prompt and the “steps in words” state is only for errors. It changes frame 4 and the “ask once” rule, and can only be answered on a real device.
  3. Does iOS have a separate switch for verification codes? If yes, Setup gets its second button (frame 12) and the first-run step may need a line about it. If no, drop the button and the footer.
  4. Should Setup carry a warning mark while AutoFill is off? (frame 24) Recommended: no, plain status as in frame 11. The card is the reminder and the mark would repeat it. Choose the variant if people who pressed Later still miss that nothing fills.
  5. Do the card’s steps need to mention the system’s master switch? If “AutoFill Passwords and Passkeys” is off, Psss may not be listed or switchable (section 9). Only a device can say. If so, the steps in AutoFillCopy.steps gain a clause and frames 4 and 16 change.
  6. Does the button land on the AutoFill pane on macOS 14 and 15? The Mac steps in words cover a miss, but the card’s button would then look broken. Device only (C6).
  7. Does the system passcode sheet appear in the provider, and what does it say? (frame 27) The key is stored with “Face ID or passcode” as its check. Whether the provider shows the sheet by itself, whether a Mac needs the login password in the same way, and whether Psss can set the prompt text can only be answered on a device. Until then frame 27 and the Mac row are the plan.
